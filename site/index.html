A newspaper with one reader

Your Day, Printed on Your Machine

Every morning, The Localhost Times reads your inbox, today's calendar, your tasks and the news, then hands you a one-page newspaper and a three-minute audio briefing to play while you brush your teeth.

It's written by Gemma 4, an open-weight model running on your own laptop through Ollama. Your inbox never leaves the house.

p. 2How it's madeThe Localhost Times

The printing press

At 06:59, the Paper Writes Itself

Four steps, one laptop, no newsroom.

At 06:59 the press wakes up and gathers the day's sources: your mail over IMAP, opened read-only so nothing gets marked as read; your calendars from their iCal links; your tasks; the local weather; and the headlines you follow.

All of it goes to Gemma 4, running on your laptop through Ollama. The model writes the edition as strict JSON that has to match a schema, and it is told never to invent a fact.

If it isn't in your sources, it isn't in your paper.

That JSON is set as a one-page newspaper and rewritten as a short script, read aloud by the voice you pick. Then the edition goes wherever you'll see it first: your inbox, your phone, or a folder on disk.

About 25 integrations, each switched on by one environment variable. The full list is on page 3.

p. 4OpinionThe Localhost Times

Editorial

Your Inbox Is Not Training Data

Most morning-briefing products work the same way. You connect your inbox, their servers read every message, and you hope the terms of service still say the same thing next year.

This paper is printed differently. The model lives on your laptop. It reads your mail there and writes there. The only thing that ever goes out is the finished script, and only if you want a cloud voice to read it.

Where your data goes
Emails and calendarRead on your laptop by Gemma 4. Never uploaded.
The finished briefing scriptSent to ElevenLabs to become a voice. Or use the macOS voice and keep it offline.
The editionDelivered through your own accounts: your mail server, your Telegram bot, your webhook.
The demo on this siteA made-up inbox for a made-up reader.

Why open models

It runs offline. Planes, trains and bad hotel Wi-Fi are all fine.

It costs $0 a morning. No API bill, no subscription.

Nobody can change the terms on you. The model is a file on your disk. No one can reprice it, retire it or rewrite its policy overnight.

Circulation

Subscriptions Are Free. Delivery Is Up to You.

You'll need Python, uv and Ollama. Five commands later, you're holding today's paper.

Quick start
brew install ollama && ollama pull gemma4:e4b
git clone https://github.com/mohamedaminehamdi/the-localhost-times
cd the-localhost-times && cp .env.example .env
uv run localhost-times --list   # see what's switched on
uv run localhost-times          # print today's edition
Delivered at 7 every morning (cron)
0 7 * * * cd ~/the-localhost-times && uv run localhost-times
